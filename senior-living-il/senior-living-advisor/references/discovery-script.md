# Linda's Discovery Script

The questions Linda asks in the first 15-20 minutes with a family, in order. Use this when designing intake forms, web-inquiry follow-up flows, or the conversation script for a sales counsellor.

The order matters. Each question opens space for the next; reordered, the conversation feels transactional.

## The questions

### 1. "Tell me about your mom. Not the medical stuff — what's she like?"

**Purpose:** signals that mom is the customer. Surfaces details Linda will use for the rest of the conversation.

**Listen for:** her name, what she did for work, hobbies, what she's lost recently, what she still does well. Write these down.

**Bad reframes to avoid:**

- "What are mom's needs?" — transactional, treats mom as a checklist
- "What's her care level?" — clinical, signals AL framing
- "How is she doing?" — too open; invites a status report instead of a portrait

### 2. "What's prompting you to look now?"

**Purpose:** finds the triggering event without forcing the family to lead with their worst moment.

**Listen for:** recent loss (spouse, friend, mobility), a specific incident (fall, missed pills, lost car keys), the family's own life change (Sandra's new role, a grandchild).

**Bad reframes to avoid:**

- "What happened?" — too direct
- "Why now?" — interrogative, makes Sandra defensive
- "Is there a crisis?" — puts a label on something that may not have one yet

### 3. "Who else is part of this decision?"

**Purpose:** maps the family system. Predicts where the friction will be.

**Listen for:** siblings (engaged, disengaged, opposed), mom herself (involved? unaware? resisting?), a spouse, a financial planner, a doctor.

**Bad reframes to avoid:**

- "Are you the decision-maker?" — treats the buyer as a procurement officer
- "Who has POA?" — too early for legal framing; surfaces it eventually but not first

### 4. "Has she toured anywhere yet, or are we early in this?"

**Purpose:** locates Sandra on the journey. A first-tour family needs different info than a fourth-tour family.

**Listen for:** what she's seen, what she liked, what put her off, what she's comparing.

**Bad reframes to avoid:**

- "Have you visited our competitors?" — frames it as a competition
- "Where else are you looking?" — makes Sandra feel surveilled

### 5. "What does the budget conversation look like in your family right now?"

**Purpose:** opens money without making the family pitch their own affordability.

**Listen for:** "we have dad's life insurance" / "we haven't talked about it" / "we don't have a lot of margin." Don't push past the answer.

**Bad reframes to avoid:**

- "What's your monthly budget?" — forces an answer they may not have
- "Can you afford X?" — patronizing
- "What's your price range?" — sounds like real estate

### 6. "If we look back at this in six months and it's working, what would that mean?"

**Purpose:** surfaces success criteria the family can actually evaluate against later.

**Listen for:** "she's made a friend" / "she's eating better" / "I sleep at night" / "she's still herself."

These become the measurable promises Linda makes — and the things she sends weekly updates about after move-in.

**Bad reframes to avoid:**

- "What are your goals?" — corporate framing
- "How will you measure success?" — makes a human transition sound like a KPI review

## After the questions

Linda restates what she heard in 3-4 sentences, **by name**, before saying anything else about the community.

> So Eleanor was a teacher for 38 years, lost John in September, and you noticed last week that she missed some Tuesday pills. Karen is 10 minutes away, Mark is in Toronto. You're trying to find a place where she'll have a friend and you can sleep at night.

Then — and only then — does she start to describe the community.

## Designing intake from this script

Direct implications for app surfaces:

- A web inquiry form that asks all six questions in order is **wrong** (too much, too soon). One question is right: "Tell me a bit about who you're looking for." The rest happens in conversation.
- A tour-booking flow should ask exactly what's needed to book the tour, plus an optional "anything you'd like me to know about your mom before we meet" field.
- The first reply to an inquiry should ask **question 1 only**, by name from a real person, with a direct phone number.
- A sales counsellor's CRM should surface these six fields as the primary record of a family — not the prospect's contact details and lead source.
- The discovery answers, with mom's name and specifics, should follow the family through every subsequent touch: tour, follow-up email, pricing conversation, move-in. "Eleanor's daughter Sandra" beats "lead #2847" everywhere it appears.

## What this script is not

This is not a sales qualification framework. The questions don't filter out unqualified prospects — they surface fit, including for prospects who shouldn't be sold to today. Linda asks the same questions of the family she'll close next week and the family she'll refer to AL elsewhere.

# Sandra, Adult Daughter Persona (extended reference)

This file is loaded by `senior-living-advisor` when working on features that benefit from deeper buyer context: move-in coordination flows, family-portal copy, sibling-collaboration features, and the post-move-in transparency feed.

## Quick facts

- Age 54, eldest of three siblings
- Marketing manager at a mid-size firm, 50 hours/week
- Mother of two: 24 and 21
- Lives in Saanich, 20 minutes from mom's house in Royal Oak (Victoria)
- Mom's name: Eleanor. 81. Widow as of 8 months ago.

## Mom (Eleanor)

- Retired teacher, 38 years in the same house in Royal Oak
- Recently widowed (husband John died of a stroke last fall)
- Still drives — short distances, in daylight
- Gardener. Reader. Belonged to a knitting group; attendance dropping
- Has osteoporosis but mobile. Forgets some pills sometimes — Sandra found a half-empty Tuesday slot last visit
- Refuses to talk about moving. "I'm fine."

## Sandra's day

- Up at 6:30, kids out by 8
- Work 8:30-6:30 with a lunch break she actually uses to take phone calls about mom
- Drives by mom's after work twice a week
- Spouse is supportive but external; mom is "Sandra's department"
- Spent the last 8 months handling dad's estate

## Sandra's siblings

- **Mark**, 51, Toronto. Phone calls only. "Whatever you decide, sis, I'll back you." Sends occasional well-meaning emails with articles he hasn't read fully.
- **Karen**, 47, lives 10 minutes from mom. Visits mom for lunch on Sundays. Resists the move-in conversation. Has more direct access to mom but less direct conversation about it. Slight friction with Sandra over "doing too much" and "rushing this."

## What Sandra is afraid of

1. That she's pushing this because _she's_ tired, not because mom needs it
2. That mom will resent her
3. That mom will deteriorate faster once she leaves the house she shared with dad for 38 years
4. That she'll pick wrong and it will be impossible to undo
5. That Karen will be right (and that Karen will say so loudly)
6. That her own kids will face this someday and watch how she handled it

## What Sandra is good at

- Spreadsheets. Comparison tables. Notes.
- Asking direct questions when she trusts you
- Following through

## What Sandra hasn't said out loud

- That she's afraid mom is grieving badly enough to be unsafe
- That she resents that this is hers to handle
- That she's already started imagining mom's room in the new place and feels guilty about that

## How to speak to her

- Acknowledge the spreadsheet. ("I can tell you've done your homework.")
- Don't congratulate her on being a good daughter. She knows. The praise lands as exposure.
- Treat the siblings' positions as data, not drama.
- Surface mom as a person — her name, her preferences, her garden.
- Always make one specific next-action the focus. Not the decision tree.

## What Sandra would tell you if you asked at month 6

"The thing that mattered most wasn't the suite or the price. It was that someone there saw mom as Eleanor and not as room 215."

## Implications for app design

Use this persona when designing:

- The family portal copy and information architecture
- Sibling-collaboration features ("send to Mark and Karen" must work for both)
- Move-in coordination (the 30-day countdown should reduce her load, not add a task list)
- Post-move-in "moments" feed (stories about Eleanor, not engagement scores)
- The pricing page (Sandra is the one comparing, so all-in numbers and add-on transparency matter)
- The web inquiry response (the first email should ask one question about Eleanor, not pitch the community)
